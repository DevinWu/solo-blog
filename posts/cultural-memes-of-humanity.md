## Who Are We?

When asked "Who are you?", people usually think of their biological individuality—their physical body, name, and daily life experiences.

Yet beyond our biological selves exists a broader, abstract identity. While less tangible than physical form, it exerts an equally profound influence on our decisions, values, and worldviews. 

This abstract identity consists of **Human Cultural Memes**.

---

## Biological Genes vs. Cultural Memes

In *The Selfish Gene*, evolutionary biologist Richard Dawkins coined the term **Meme** to describe units of cultural transmission. 

- **Genes** replicate through biological reproduction, passing down physical traits across generations.
- **Memes** replicate through language, art, philosophy, code, and ideas, passing down knowledge and cultural values across human consciousness.

Our physical bodies are temporary vessels for genetic information. In contrast, the books we write, the code we open-source, the stories we tell, and the values we uphold survive far beyond our mortal lifespans.

---

## Identity in the Information Age

As software engineers and creators, we write algorithms and build systems that interact with millions of minds worldwide. 

In a very real sense:
> We are not merely defined by our physical presence, but by the cultural and intellectual memes we nurture and transmit to future generations.
